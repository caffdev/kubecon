Last mechanism: how a version moves from dev to stage to prod. Today that's a person editing YAML in another repo. It becomes a pull request. On the left, GitOps Promoter, our lean, and stage C. Each environment is a branch, and a promotion is a commit and a pull request. There's no state outside Git, so the PR is the audit record that a reviewer, an agent and an auditor all read. Notice what that does to change control: the approval stops being a click and becomes a review. On the right, Kargo, with its own control plane and UI. It's a serious option, and Meta runs Argo CD and Kargo across about a hundred clusters. For us it would be a second control plane. What would flip it, said out loud so the decision looks as reversible as it is: Promoter's rollback per environment is hand-wired, it doesn't order across apps, and it's younger. Every published comparison is written by someone with a stake, so the answer is a two-week spike on one real app. We don't quote any Kargo adoption figure; the one in our notes came from a vendor post. So, if all of this exists, why haven't our teams asked for any of it?
