ABCDE
Two stages on one slide, because they're one piece of work from a funding point of view. The distinction to hold onto is commitment versus proof of concept. Stage C is a commitment. A spike on Argo's source hydrator first, then GitOps Promoter on one app: dev to prod with nobody editing YAML. Agent-or-human attribution goes in the same stage, because it's cheap now and expensive to retrofit. And separation of duties in machinery we already run: whoever proposes a change can't approve its own pull request, the forge enforces the rules, and RBAC bounds each role. The honest gap: revoking a permission doesn't undo merges that already happened. Withdrawal isn't rollback. Stage D is one commitment and one proof of concept. The commitment is fleet waves: ApplicationSets with rolling sync and a health gate between waves. The proof of concept is canary: two patterns, one pilot service each, time-boxed, and "neither is worth it yet" is an allowed answer. Even the proof of concept has automated analysis. And a kill switch is not a rollback. Progressive delivery reverts a version; it can't turn off one behaviour inside a version. That's a feature flag's job, and CVS Health, the company we opened with, has the answer to that too. The exit gate is deliberately brutal: a health gate nobody has watched fail is a health gate nobody should trust. And then, the summit.
