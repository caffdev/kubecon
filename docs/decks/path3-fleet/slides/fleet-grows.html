Our shape: regulated, multi-cluster EKS plus on-prem

Clusters, from each company's own talk. Log scale. ⚠️ Hertz's 80 is from the room. The arrow is a direction, not a count.

They weren't unusual. These are the fleet sizes companies disclosed from the stage this year, each from their own title, abstract or deck, with one exception. Salesforce, a thousand clusters. LinkedIn, three hundred, on bare metal. Meta, about a hundred, run by about fifteen engineers. Hertz, eighty. Coinbase, seventy-nine. Mastercard, thirty EKS clusters. Verkada, nine. It's a log scale so the small ones still show. None of them is single-cluster, and none of them upgrades by hand. The bracket is us: regulated, EKS plus on-prem, in the middle of the table next to Hertz, Coinbase and Mastercard. Our split isn't an awkward halfway state. It's the normal shape of a fleet our size. Click. And we're climbing. Look at what we already brought home as the plan: live-live across two regions, agent pods and their sandboxes from Path 2, GPU pools, on-prem sites joining through Hybrid Nodes, Crossplane writing app infrastructure, plus the Argo and canary work from Path 1. Every one of those lands on Kubernetes. None of it runs on ECS. So the fleet grows whatever we decide. If anyone asks about Hertz: we heard eighty in the room; their abstract only says "fleet scale". Everything else is public. Which makes the question we keep getting, "EKS or ECS?", the wrong question.
