NVIDIA, "5,000 Nodes in Minutes, Twice in a Week" (deck-assets/12-fleet-node-changes). Illustrative status mix.

The third design rule has nothing to do with AI, and it's the best template we saw all week for anything we'll later want a machine to drive. Ship every change together with the check that fails without it. NVIDIA had two kernel CVEs to fix, twice in one week, across EKS, GKE, AKS, OKE and on-prem. The fix shipped with its own check, the check fails until the fix has really landed on that node, and every node reports a status. So the picture on the right is the audit. Every square is a node, about fifty-six hundred of them. Most are verified, a few are still pending, and one failed and is being retried. Where the fleet stands is a kubectl get, not a spreadsheet someone fills in. They converged about fifty-six hundred nodes in thirty minutes. The status mix here is illustrative; the pattern is theirs. That's fleet toil. Multi-cluster has a second problem that looks similar and isn't.
