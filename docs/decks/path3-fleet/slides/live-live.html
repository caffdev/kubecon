"No user-visible downtime" isn't a number. It's a sum, and the slowest part of it is what the customers see. Four layers. Delivery is solved: ApplicationSets already put the same app in both regions, so a scheduler buys us nothing here. Traffic: something has to notice the region is gone, and the change has to propagate. Then clients keep the old DNS answer cached for as long as they like, which lands inside our recovery time and is out of our control. That's why we recommend AWS Global Accelerator at the edge: it takes the DNS cache off the clock. That's our recommendation, to be clear, not something we saw presented. State: we can't fail traffic over faster than the data. Aurora Global or DynamoDB global tables have to converge in the surviving region, and doing it in the other order is how writes split. Capacity: the survivor inherits a hundred percent of traffic at fifty percent of its size. Either we pay for permanent double headroom, and this lane is zero, or Karpenter's scale-up time is formally part of our RTO. The lengths are illustrative. Measuring them is decision 2b. And the developer-facing half of multi-cluster is the part nobody budgets.
