Cattle still need upgrading, and the second design rule is that upgrade cadence is a policy, not a project. Every EKS minor version gets fourteen months of standard support. Inside that window, the control plane costs about ten cents an hour per cluster, around seventy-three dollars a month. Miss the window and the cluster drops into extended support, at about sixty cents an hour, around four hundred and forty dollars a month, per cluster, six times as much. Those figures come from third-party write-ups, so we check the pricing page before quoting them in a meeting. But the shape is the point: a missed upgrade is now a line on the bill, not just a risk. So the rule is: never enter extended support. Every cluster gets upgraded inside its window and has a named owner. Skip-level upgrades help, three minors in one rollout instead of six waves, which is what LinkedIn did. And we should measure our own toil, hours per EKS minor upgrade last year, so this argument carries our number and not just theirs. And every upgrade change carries its own proof.
