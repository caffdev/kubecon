Stage 4 needs one line drawn before anyone builds on it. Mark the seam before we pour. Read the building from the ground up. The landing zone: accounts, guardrails, org-wide scaffolding. Then CDK: VPCs, the EKS clusters themselves, shared services. Both of those change rarely. Then the seam, the dashed line, where ownership hands off. Above it, Crossplane and kro, starting with S3 and moving to the infrastructure app teams own. And at the top, apps and agents, which call narrow, typed APIs rather than writing raw cloud YAML. An agent calls a typed API far more reliably than it writes CloudFormation. Below the seam things change rarely; above it they change constantly. Two tools fighting over one resource starts with an unmarked line, and once it's poured, it's expensive to move. This is decision 3, and it needs a diagram with the seam marked, not a debate. Nice moment from the show: two of the Crossplane contribfest slides were our own function-lifecycle work. And above the seam, developers still have no road.
