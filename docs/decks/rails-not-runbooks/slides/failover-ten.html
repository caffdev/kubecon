Lose a region and the app doesn’t move:
traffic, capacity and data do.

GOALS §5.1 B (live-live in four layers). Karmada: Bloomberg, Karmada Updates and Use Cases (no deck). No seconds on purpose: each segment is measured at a game day.

Here's what the rails buy the people who write the apps. A developer never names a cluster. The app's repo says where it runs as labels: production, both AWS regions. The ApplicationSet finds every cluster with those labels. So the app already runs in both regions, three replicas each, and the thirtieth cluster costs the developer nothing. Now lose a region. Four layers, two AWS regions, and the second cloud on the right. Click. Region one fails. Detection starts the clock: health checks have to fail enough times to be believed. Click. Traffic moves: Global Accelerator at the edge, Route 53 behind it, Istio locality failover between services. All of it lands on region two. Nobody redeployed anything. Click. Capacity. Region two was sized for half the traffic and just got all of it. The autoscaler doubles the pods and Auto Mode adds nodes. Minutes, and part of our recovery time whether we budget for it or not. Click. State. Region two's database replica is promoted to writer. The slowest layer, and not a Kubernetes problem at all. So recovery time is a sum, and the data sets it. No seconds on the slide on purpose: we measure each one at a game day. On rails, failover isn't a runbook someone opens at three in the morning. It's labels, and four layers that are already running. And if all of AWS goes, the second cloud holds a warm standby. How data crosses clouds is undecided, and the slide says so. That's the fleet half: clusters, tools and apps, all on rails.
