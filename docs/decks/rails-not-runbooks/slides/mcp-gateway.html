Stacklok + AWS, Cedar Analysis for MCP Servers. Mastercard, No RAG, No Regrets. Entity names in the policy are ours.

As users, agents work through tools, and these days that mostly means MCP. Stacklok and AWS framed MCP auth as three questions. Who is calling, and how do they prove it? For us, the agent's own workload identity, not a person's borrowed token. Who decides what it may call? A policy at the gateway, groups from the directory. What identity reaches the tool? A token minted for that one call, never the agent's. On the right, question two as a Cedar policy: triage agents may call read-only tools on the Kubernetes server. Cedar can be analysed, so you can prove what a policy set allows before any agent tries it. Click. The choice is the gateway, Obot, ToolHive or our own thin proxy, decided in a short bake-off on where enforcement sits. And the dissent: Mastercard dropped MCP for narrow built-in tools, and drives thirty EKS clusters that way. The scoped token in question three is the whole credentials story.
