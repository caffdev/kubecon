That's on-prem from code. LinkedIn went further, at about ten times our size: more than two hundred and fifty clusters, around half a million nodes, on their own hardware. This is their result slide. They took production from Kubernetes 1.31 to 1.34. Three minors. The old way, one minor at a time, was six fleet waves and forty business days. They did it in one rollout and a promotion, in seven. The trick is in the title of their talk: binary version is not behaviour. It's KEP-4330, compatibility versions. You deploy the 1.34 binaries, but pinned with the emulated-version flag, so they behave like 1.31. You soak that. If anything's wrong, rollback is just a binary downgrade, because behaviour never changed. Then you release, by raising the emulated version. One promotion instead of three upgrades. Two honest notes. LinkedIn did this with their own control-plane tooling, so before we promise it we check what kubeadm and Cluster API support. And this removes minor-version toil, not patching: security patches still ship every month, through the waves we're about to build. That's on-prem. In AWS, the cluster itself comes from code.
