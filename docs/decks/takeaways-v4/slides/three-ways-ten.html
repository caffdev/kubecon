What answers itour agents, through the waves
Now the show's biggest topic. Two hundred and twenty-eight of five hundred and one talks were about AI, nearly half, and they disagreed: half the stages had AI solving problems, the other half had it creating them. Our third takeaway: AI brings problems as it solves them. On our map, agents don't arrive as one thing. They arrive three ways at once, and each needs a different answer. Click. As workloads. An agent is a pod on our nodes, beside our apps, often running code it wrote a minute ago. Podium runs every coding agent as an isolated pod, and those agents open thirty percent of their merged pull requests. Click. As users. The same agent calls our tools: it opens pull requests, asks for infrastructure, reads the cluster. Mastercard lets an LLM drive thirty EKS clusters, with every write reviewed. Click. And as attackers, the one nobody puts on a roadmap. They point their agents at the open source we run. One Metal3 maintainer's own AI pipeline found two hundred and twenty issues in a year, twenty-two of them security bugs, and attackers have the same models. We can't fence that out. We have to out-pace it. The first two have the same answer, because they're the same object: a Job.
