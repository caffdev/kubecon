Rule two: mark the seam before we pour. This is our AWS stack drawn as a building in section. The foundation is the Landing Zone Accelerator: accounts and guardrails. Then CDK: VPCs, the EKS clusters, shared services. Then the seam, the amber line. Above it, Crossplane, starting with S3 and moving to typed APIs that apps, and agents, can call. Then the apps themselves. Read the arrows on the left. Below the seam, things change rarely. Above it, they change constantly. Two tools fighting over one AWS resource always starts with a line nobody drew. And like concrete, once it's poured, moving it is expensive. The title block says what it costs: decision three, and what it needs is a diagram with the seam marked. A meeting, not a purchase. American Family Insurance with Upbound showed Crossplane once adoption is real, and Meta showed the move from Terraform on a cron job to GitOps. And under the foundation is the one thing we can't get back.
