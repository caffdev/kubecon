Rules four and five share a slide, because they're one idea: nothing leaves the station without a signal, whoever's driving. Promotion really is a train leaving one station for the next: dev, stage, prod. And a departure board is a public, timestamped record of what left and when, which is what we want GitOps to be. Read the board. LinkedIn: canary namespaces with an Argo CD PostSync gate took a release from a week to an hour. Transfix: agents earn the right to merge, from shadow mode to a granted permission. Ford: CI as the safety net under GitOps that AI wrote, across hundreds of clusters. And our row, in amber: Kargo or GitOps Promoter. That's decision one, and it's held for a two-week spike on one app, not more blog posts. Now the bottom of the board, the standing notice. It's the policy rule, and it never names a person or an agent. No privileged pods, no host mounts, no unapproved model or tool, whoever wrote the change. It runs in CI first, so a violation is a failing test an agent can fix, and then at admission in CEL, with no webhook in the path. AT&T, Google, JPMorganChase, Grainger and Nirmata all showed versions of it. Write the rule against the action, not the actor, and agent governance mostly comes free. The signal is the pipeline, not a person reading every diff. But the drivers that aren't people need more than a rule. They need a boundary.
