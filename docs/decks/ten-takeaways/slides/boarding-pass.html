Illustrative pass

Rule six: boarding passes, not house keys. A house key works forever, for whoever holds it. That's what a long-lived secret is, and a secret an agent can read is a secret that can leave the building. A boarding pass names who you are, is good for one trip, and is worthless an hour later. That's workload identity. This pass is illustrative: a triage agent in the SRE namespace, a read-only seat, going to the metrics API, with a SPIFFE identity, valid for fifteen minutes, checked by Cedar or OpenFGA, carrying a short-lived certificate, and no static secret on file. On the stub, the other passengers on this flight at the show: Coinbase moving from bearer tokens to mesh mTLS, Google and Microsoft on pod certificates, RBC on why Kubernetes Secrets were a bad idea, and two more. An identity says who the agent is. It doesn't say where it's allowed to run.
