Here's the one sentence for the whole talk. We can build, from the first EKS cluster, what the big operators had to retrofit. The curves are illustrative. The black line is the work: every cluster, times every release, times every CVE. The red line is the team, and teams grow one hire at a time. The blue gap is toil: somebody does it by hand, or it doesn't get done. LinkedIn put a number on it: three Kubernetes minors across two hundred and fifty clusters, one at a time, is forty business days. Click. Everyone we heard from hit the bend first, then rebuilt the platform while it was running. Revision A, Coinbase: a homegrown auth system replaced with SPIFFE across four thousand services. Click. B, Cisco Meraki: a twenty-year monolith, moved onto a new contract between platform and app teams. Click. C, Nutanix: one deleted object pruned thirty-nine production clusters, and it took four days to come back. Click. Revision D is us, and it isn't drawn yet. We're here, before the bend. We get to decide how a cluster, a service and an agent are made before there are a hundred of them. So what does "built for it from day one" look like by 2030?
