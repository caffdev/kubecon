Sheet two: apps on a contract. On the left is LinkedIn's real deployment spec. That's all an app team writes: a name, replicas, a version, CPU and memory. About ten lines. The platform owns everything else: pod shape, injected containers, security, placement. Then there's one road to production. We're keeping Argo CD basic, with no promotion tools and no progressive sync, so safety comes from two boring gates. Click. Gate one is CI before merge: render, schema, policy. Ford runs hundreds of clusters this way, with pull requests from people, AI and bots, and reviewers read the content, not the syntax. Click. Gate two is after the sync: a PostSync hook, which is just a Job with one annotation. It smoke-tests what just deployed. Pass, and the next wave goes. Fail, and a person gets paged; that's the red branch. LinkedIn went from a week of hoping to an hour of proof. Click. Ours: one golden Helm chart and a short values file. The move to EKS is the moment to impose it. An app moves on the contract, or it doesn't move yet. Now the pods are running. Who are they, and what are they allowed to touch?
