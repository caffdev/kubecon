Here's the cost of hitting that wall late. Think of it as a revision table on a drawing: the changes you make after it's built. Click. Revision A, Coinbase. Their service-to-service auth was homegrown and tightly tied to AWS. They replaced it with SPIFFE across more than four thousand services, and after a long tail of adoption, retired the old system in March 2025. Click. Revision B, Cisco Meraki. A twenty-year-old monolith: five hundred services, a thousand database shards, five thousand hand-provisioned servers. Moving to Kubernetes meant writing a new contract between the platform and every app team. Click. Revision C, Nutanix. One deleted object at the top of their GitOps tree pruned all thirty-nine production clusters. Four days to recover, and the backups were twelve hours old and had never been tested. Click. Revision D is us, and it isn't drawn yet. That's the point. The tax they paid is the tax we skip, if we build these things in from the first cluster. So what does "built in from day one" look like by 2030?
