Move two: apps on a contract. On the left is LinkedIn's real deployment spec. That's all an app team writes: a name, a replica count, a version, CPU and memory. About ten lines. On the right is what the platform owns, which is everything else: the shape of the pod spec, the init containers it injects, the runtime contract, volumes, security, placement, and a policy that stops teams setting their own node selectors. Behind those ten lines LinkedIn runs five million pods and a hundred and ten thousand deploys a week. Click. They aren't alone. Cisco Meraki's migration talk called Kubernetes a new platform contract, not an infrastructure swap. Meta replaced hand-written YAML with an order form. ŌURA cut their customer-facing code by about ninety-three percent. Click. Ours: one golden Helm chart, a short values file, and one plain Argo CD Application per app. The migration to EKS is the moment to impose it. An app moves on the contract, or it doesn't move yet. If app teams write ten lines, something has to catch the bad ones. With plain Argo CD, that's CI.
