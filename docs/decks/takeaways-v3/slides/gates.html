This is the slide the whole second half has been building to. Read it from the middle out. The middle is the test cluster. Then one canary cluster. Then the prod fleet in waves: wave one, wave two, and the rest of prod. A new version of a platform tool starts in the middle and moves outward one ring at a time, and between every ring there's a gate. Click. A new Prometheus goes onto the test cluster. Its gate passes: targets are up, rules are evaluating, nothing's dropped. So it moves out. Click. It reaches the canary cluster, which is a real cluster with real workloads. And this time the gate fails: targets dropped after the upgrade. Click. And here's the point. Nobody decides anything. The sync fails, so the rollout stops right there. Wave one never starts. The prod rings never see it. Nobody gets paged, and the team from the start of this talk finds out from a message in the morning, not a phone call at night. Then they fix forward, or roll the canary back, and replay the gate. On the right: each tool brings its own gate. Argo CD checks every app is still synced and healthy. Prometheus checks its own targets and rules. The OTel collector checks that exports are succeeding. Karpenter checks that pending pods still get nodes. Different checks, same machinery. This isn't our invention. LinkedIn showed it for their cluster services: the gate is just an Argo CD PostSync job. Exit zero, the wave advances. Exit one, the sync fails and the rollout stops before prod, with the failed job and its logs still there to read. They took a release from about a week of manual testing to about an hour. And the pipeline also needs to know when not to run at all.
