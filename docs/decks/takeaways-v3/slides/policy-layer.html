One policy layer checks every change, whether a person or an agent made it.

The same rules, for all three

  • No privileged podswhoever wrote the manifest
  • Egress to approved models and MCP toolsnothing else leaves the cluster
  • Nothing outside the change windowthe window from the change record
Identity says who the agent is. Policy says what anyone may do. And "anyone" is the point. Three kinds of actor change our clusters now. A person with kubectl. The pipeline, which is how most change should arrive. And agents. All three go through the same gate: Kyverno, with the same rules, checked twice. Once in CI, where a failing rule is a fast test failure the author can fix, person or agent. And again at admission, so nothing gets in around the pipeline. The rules are written against the action, not the actor. No privileged pods, whoever wrote the manifest. Egress only to approved models and approved MCP tools. Nothing outside the change window, the same window the change record opened. Write them that way and agent governance mostly comes for free. That's the fence along the bottom of the range: one gate on every trail, whoever's walking it. It's the second thing that crosses all three paths. The first is next. Policy stops what shouldn't happen. Telemetry shows what did.
