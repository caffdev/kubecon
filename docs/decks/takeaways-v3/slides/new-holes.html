Now the third way agents arrive: in attackers' hands. This is every CVE published, year by year. For a decade it climbed steadily: ten thousand, twenty, thirty, forty-five thousand last year. And this year, seventy-nine thousand, with the year not over. That's over two hundred new holes a day. What changed is AI. Metal3's security lead at Ericsson told this on stage: in 2026, every vulnerability disclosure Metal3 received came from his own AI pipeline. Two hundred and twenty findings in one small project. Most were noise: duplicates, hardening, things that weren't real. Twenty-two were real. And he's a defender. Mythos vs Maintainers was the other side: attackers pointing the same models at the same code, and maintainers drowning in reports that look terrifying and often aren't. Click. Now put that against how we patch. Remember the runbook: three upgrades a year. A hole that lands on a random day waits, on average, half the gap to the next one. Sixty-one days open, against exploits that get written in hours. His first takeaway, word for word: "AI finds bugs fast, and not only for us. Shorten your upgrade cycle." We built the shorter cycle in the first half. The waves, the gates, the windows. So the question is who drives it.
