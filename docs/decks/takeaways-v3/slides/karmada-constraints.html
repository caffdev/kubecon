Now the developers. Their hidden hours went on three things: knowing what runs where and why, picking clusters and regions, and keeping DR patterns alive. Today, somebody decides which cluster each app goes to, and writes that cluster's name down. Here's the north-star alternative. On the left is everything a developer writes. Constraints: it runs in the cloud, it's PCI, it stays in the US. And asks: six replicas, spread across two regions, and fall back to the second cloud if it has to. There isn't a cluster name anywhere. On the right, Karmada does the placing. It reads the labels every cluster carries, its provider, its region, its compliance scope, and how much room it has, and it spreads the six replicas over the clusters that fit. Three in region one, three in region two. The second cloud is held as the fallback. To be fair about it: for apps whose placement never changes, Argo CD's ApplicationSets can already select clusters by label. Karmada is the option that goes further, because it places by capacity and it keeps placing when things change. And that's the part that matters on a bad day. Because the constraints keep holding when the clusters underneath them don't.
