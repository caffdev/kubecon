Pattern: LinkedIn’s canary pipeline; each gate is an Argo CD PostSync Job.

The four tools in that chart were the demo. Our platform really runs twenty: Argo CD, Prometheus, the Splunk collector, Vault, Ceph, Istio and the rest. Twenty tools, four releases a year, on every cluster that runs them: about sixteen hundred and seventy rollouts a year by hand. Through one pipeline, it's eighty approvals. The team stops doing rollouts and starts approving them. Here's the pipeline. A platform tool ships like an app. A new version starts on the test cluster in the middle and moves out one ring at a time: a canary, then wave one, wave two, the rest of prod. Clusters join a wave by label, which is why the region claim labelled them. Click. Between every ring there's a gate, the tool's own check. Say a new Prometheus passes on test, reaches the canary, and the gate fails: targets dropped. Click. Nobody decides anything. The sync fails, the rollout stops, wave one never starts. Nobody is paged; the team fixes forward in the morning or rolls the canary back. And a wave that reaches Thursday's close waits for Monday on its own. That's LinkedIn's pattern: each gate is an Argo CD PostSync job, and it took their releases from about a week of manual testing to about an hour. Clusters from code, every change through one pipeline. Now the day it matters.
