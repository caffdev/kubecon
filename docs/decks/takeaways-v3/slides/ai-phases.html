With all of that in place, here's how far we let agents climb, and when. Six camps. Camp zero is where we are: people using AI on their own machines, and a person applying every change. Camp one, by the second quarter of 2027: alert triage. For every alert, an agent explains it: is it real, how serious, what changed, where to look. It explains; it never acts. It needs the one view and the lake we just saw, and nothing else, because it has no write path. That's why it's first, and why the date is safe. Verkada runs this at thirty-three cents an alert. Camp two, by the end of 2027: agents propose pull requests. A fix, a resize, a config change, always as a PR, through review, policy in CI and the same pipeline as everyone else. The resize we just saw starts here. Camps three to five are the north star, and they're undated on purpose. Always-on analysis, where the agent owns alert severity. Agents that fix things themselves, inside a blast radius we drew, with the sandbox, the gates, rollback and the change windows all in place. And teams writing their own agents, a DBA agent and an SRE agent working the same incident. Each camp has a gate, written underneath it, and we don't move up until the gate is real.
