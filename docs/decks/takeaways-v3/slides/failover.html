Remember "fingers crossed"? Here's the day it matters. At the bottom is all a developer writes: the cloud, PCI, US; six replicas over two regions, and a fallback. No cluster names. The platform matches them against cluster labels, the labels the region claim handed out. So the app runs in both AWS regions, three and three, both live. Click. Region one goes down. Region two already runs half the app, so traffic shifts there and Auto Mode adds the nodes for the rest. Click. Now AWS itself has the bad day. The constraints say fall back to the second cloud, so a warm standby there, placed by the same labels, scales up and takes the traffic. Click. And nobody edited a file. Nobody rewrote a list of clusters at two in the morning. Fingers uncrossed. To be straight about how. Nothing moves: traffic, capacity and data fail over. Both regions live and placed by labels is Argo CD ApplicationSets; we can do that now. The second cloud needs a warm standby, or something that moves work on its own. Karmada is that option, Bloomberg runs on it, and it earns its place with a spike, not a mandate. And traffic moves in seconds, data doesn't: our recovery time is the sum of noticing, moving traffic, scaling up and promoting the database, so the slowest layer sets it. That's the fleet.
