Agents as workloads, first: how they spawn. This is one day, hour by hour. The pale band is our elastic node count: Karpenter or Auto Mode adding nodes when there's work and taking them away when there isn't. Each teal diamond is one agent, spawned for one job, gone when it's done. At one in the morning a schedule fires: the nightly agents that check drift and costs. At three, an alert: a handful of triage agents spin up, look, report and vanish. And through the working day, a steady stream: agents reviewing pull requests, drafting changes, answering questions about the platform. Look at the corner. A couple of hundred agents in a day, and not one of them was approved by a person, one by one. That's the point of them. Nobody wants to approve two hundred agents. But it means each one is a user of our platform that nobody looked at before it started. The numbers are illustrative; the shape is what our Jenkins-triggered agent pods will look like. And every one of them runs code it may have just written.
