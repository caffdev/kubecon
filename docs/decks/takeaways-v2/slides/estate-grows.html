Six reasonable steps turned two clusters into twenty-eight. One person still upgrades them.

day one+ DR+ EKS+ teams+ AI+ 2nd cloud

We started small. A handful of people, two clusters on our own hardware, built with kubeadm by hand. One to test on, one to run on. And one person who upgrades them. Click. Prod got important enough to need a twin, so DR joined. And DR only works if it matches prod version for version, so every upgrade now happens twice, in lockstep. Click. Then AWS. A dev cluster and two prod clusters in two regions. EKS is a different animal: AWS runs the control plane, the rest is on us, and none of it looks like the kubeadm runbook. Two kinds of cluster, two runbooks. Click. Then every new team brought apps, people and, usually, a reason for a cluster of its own. Fourteen clusters. Nobody decided that; it just happened, one reasonable request at a time. Click. Then AI, two ways at once. GPU clusters for the workloads, in teal. And agents as users: the teal diamonds call our API, open pull requests and read our metrics. Mastercard has an LLM driving thirty EKS clusters; Salesforce runs agentic operations across a thousand. Click. And because one provider's bad day can't be ours, a second cloud, with apps moving into it. Nearly every large fleet at the show was hybrid or multi-cloud. Twenty-eight clusters. Three kinds of place. Every step made sense on the day. And there's still one person in orange. So what does one upgrade actually cost him?
