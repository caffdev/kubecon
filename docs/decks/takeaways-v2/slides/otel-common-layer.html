OTel isn’t beating Prometheus; it’s the one layer our classic and AI workloads share.

One more layer runs on every cluster we just rolled to, and it's the one that sees both kinds of workload. The headline is the point, so let me be careful with it. This isn't OTel winning and Prometheus losing. Prometheus stays. It's on the right, as a place metrics land, and plenty of what we run will keep exposing Prometheus metrics for years. What OTel is, is the layer in the middle. On the left, our classic workloads: services and batch jobs, emitting metrics, logs and traces. Below them, the AI workloads: model servers and agents, emitting things classic tools have no words for: token counts, tool calls, which model answered. OpenTelemetry has semantic conventions for exactly that now, and Google and Microsoft spent a whole session on keeping them consistent. Both go through the same collector on every cluster. That's the splunk-otel-collector we're already rolling out, so this isn't a new tool. And it stamps the same cluster, namespace and team on everything, classic and AI alike. That's what lets us ask one question across both: what did this team's agent cost, and what did it touch? IBM and JPMorgan showed it in practice: their inference servers emit OTel traces and get scraped by Prometheus, both at once. And everything that layer collects has to land somewhere the agents can read.
