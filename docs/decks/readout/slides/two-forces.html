Two things got loud this week, from two different directions. On the left, fleets. Companies running hundreds of clusters and still upgrading them by hand. Ninety percent of AI runs on Kubernetes, so the fleet is growing, not shrinking. On the right, agents. They're a new kind of workload that needs sandboxing, identity and egress control. And they're a new kind of user: they want to change our infrastructure, which means they need a path to production that doesn't depend on a human reading every diff. Click. Follow both routes up and they arrive at the same place. A change has to reach a cluster safely without a person reviewing every line, whether there are too many clusters for people to keep up, or the author isn't a person at all. The answer to both is one control plane: GitOps, policy, and progressive delivery. That's what makes a change safe regardless of who or what wrote it. First force first: the fleets.
