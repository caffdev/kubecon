The week in six sentences

Kubernetes became the AI control plane, and agents became its newest tenant

  1. This is an AI-infrastructure conference now. Eight of the nine Day 1 keynotes after the opening remarks were about AI workloads, and the AI Inference + Agentic track made its debut as the largest non-maintainer content track 12. My notebook from the hall: "2024 it was a dream, 2026 is mainline" 20.

  2. Agents are a workload class with a parts list. Sandboxing, identity, durable execution and tool plumbing (MCP) each had named projects, and the CNCF took two agent runtimes into its sandbox on the opening morning: Agent Substrate and OpenShell 49.

  3. The scheduler is the hard problem. Running containers is treated as solved; placing GPUs, NICs and TPUs by topology, quota and fairness is where the best engineering talks were, with Dynamic Resource Allocation (DRA) as the organizing API 23.

  4. Platforms now serve two kinds of user. Humans still matter, but the open design question is what an agent may change without a person reading the diff. The answer on stage was GitOps plus policy plus progressive delivery 12.

  5. Everyone runs fleets. Salesforce talked about 1,000 clusters, LinkedIn about 300 clusters and roughly 300,000 nodes, and Meta about roughly 100 clusters run by roughly 15 engineers. Manual upgrades do not survive that arithmetic 1.

  6. Security and observability got pulled into AI's orbit. The CVE firehose (now fed by AI-found bugs) and non-human identity dominated security; OpenTelemetry is the default, and it is being pointed at inference cost and agent behaviour 2.

What the keynotes said

Three mornings, each with its own argument. Day 1 put agents inside the cluster and started building the fence. Day 2 handed the stage to end users running at scale. Day 3 asked what Kubernetes should become when everyone customizes it.

Day 1 · Tuesday, November 10

Agents move into the cluster

The most repeated word on stage was sandbox. Speakers treated agents as workloads that need their own isolation, identity and scheduling, and the foundation shipped the first open building blocks the same morning.

09:00

Welcome + Opening Remarks

Jonathan Bryce, Chris Aniszczyk (Linux Foundation / CNCF), with guests from Anyscale, CoreWeave, Upbound and Vultr

The scoreboard: 231 projects, about 21.2 million cloud native developers (up more than 5.5 million in a year), 82,000 attendees at CNCF events over the past year, and eight new Platinum members including Adyen, CVS, NVIDIA and HAProxy 4. New sandbox projects OpenShell and Agent Substrate; llm-d reached 1.0. Two certifications are planned for 2027: Kubernetes AI Associate (KAIA) and Certified Kubernetes Inference Engineer (CKIE) 4. One recap quotes inference traffic across providers up fourfold in eight months 7 unverified.

The AI Conformance program now counts 40 certified platforms 4. TechTimes went through the public repos and found the 40 is cumulative across versions, that only four platforms had submitted against v1.37, and that the test suite names a single accelerator vendor 5. Treat the badge as a checklist, not a benchmark.

09:33

Agent Substrate: Open Layers for Agentic Systems

Jaana Dogan, Dmitry Berkovich, Tim Hockin, Eitan Yarmush, Luiz Scheidegger · Anthropic, Google, Solo.io

The headline project of the week. Its unit is the actor: one agent session paired with a sandbox and a gateway. Idle actors suspend to microVM snapshots and wake on warm worker pods, so you stop paying for a pod per session while state, identity and access controls stay with the actor 910. Isolation comes from gVisor and lightweight VMs 912. It was accepted into the CNCF on stage, and 1.0 shipped the same day 910.

Google's Tim Hockin framed the ambition as doing for agentic workloads what Kubernetes did for deployments 11. Solo.io's enterprise build puts agentgateway in front of every actor for ingress and egress policy 10.

09:42

Engineering Kubernetes for the Next Era of Workloads

Mike Stefaniak · AWS Deck posted

The only keynote that posted slides, and the most concrete ten minutes of the week: clusters approaching 100,000 nodes, 10 GB images, and accelerators "a device count can't describe," with every fix landed upstream in Kubernetes 1.37, containerd 2.2 and Karpenter 1.15 3. The slides are rebuilt below.

09:49

Building the Next Generation of AI with Open Source

Warren Barkley, Ali Golshan · NVIDIA

NVIDIA pitched itself as an upstream citizen for "AI factories." The more consequential news was OpenShell, the NVIDIA-led agent sandbox, entering the CNCF sandbox the same day. It stacks a hardened container, a microVM, and a hardware policy layer on BlueField-4 DPUs, and NVIDIA says it will integrate it with Agent Substrate 49.

"We can't simply ask agents to follow the rules; the infrastructure must enforce them."Justin Boitano, NVIDIA, speaking to TechTarget 9
09:58

Sandbox Your Agents

Jessica Forrester · Red Hat Recording

The threat model in one line from the abstract: an agent with too much access "can take down entire production stacks and wipe databases." The answer was layered isolation from community parts, Confidential Containers plus OpenShell, applied to development workflows as well as production agents 119.

10:05

Pods in the Wild: Reinforcement Learning on Kubernetes with OpenRL

Sunil Arora, Ravin Kumar · Google, Google DeepMind

DolphinGemma, an open-weight Gemma model trained on decades of dolphin recordings, was the hook. The argument: fine-tuned open-weight models beat frontier baselines on domain tasks at a fraction of the cost, and RL should leave the research lab 1. OpenRL was contributed to the CNCF 4 and schedules its RL primitives with DRA 7.

10:15

The Harness Is the New Runtime

Idit Levine, Keith Babo · Solo.io

The idea I keep thinking about. Desktop agent harnesses (a model plus a modular runtime of tools, skills and plugins) will make the same trip to shared clusters that containers made from laptops. The hard part is keeping the user experience while adopting sandboxing, checkpoint/restore, scale and identity 1. Kubernetes 1.37 added alpha pod-level checkpoint/restore, which is the primitive this needs 16.

"The next generation of applications will be agents."Idit Levine, Solo.io 10
10:22

One Kubernetes, Different Environments: Closing the Portability Gaps

Dilpreet Bindra · Broadcom

Two quiet but durable moves: VM API, a provider-neutral, Cluster-API-style VM lifecycle API, entered the CNCF sandbox, and a new Workload Conformance Working Group (with SIG Apps and SIG Architecture) will define verifiable workload behaviour so ISVs validate once 712.

10:29

The Identity Crisis: Who is the AI Agent in Your Cluster?

Abdel Sghiouar · Google Cloud

Kubernetes identity assumes deterministic workloads with known call paths. Agents generate network calls and run machine-written code on the fly, so the question "when an agent calls a tool or a database, what identity does it present?" has no good default answer yet 1. No deck was posted; the 13 sessions on SPIFFE and workload identity are the follow-up reading 2.

10:41

From Pod to Actuator: Physical AI and the Future of Kubernetes

NVIDIA (scheduled speaker: Abhilash Somasamudramath)

Robotics as a cluster workload. A recap describes a bipedal robot demo built with Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics, and a render, train, deploy-to-edge pipeline across three computers 7 unverified.

Day 2 · Wednesday, November 11

Agent speed meets end-user reality

The end users took the stage: CVS Health, Adyen, Apple, Meta and Uber. The shared message was that open, vendor-neutral platforms are how large companies stay in control of their own stack.

Human Scale, Agent Scale

Jonathan Bryce · The Linux Foundation

Systems were sized for human-paced traffic; agents do not browse like people. A recap quotes Bryce saying agents now generate 70% of tracked inference traffic 8 unverified. Elsewhere in the week he called AI "one of the largest compute workloads the industry has ever seen" 6.

Per the recap: built in about ten months, thousands of apps and tens of thousands of deployments a month, dozens of clusters presented as one logical cluster, and the CAP Registry API and a feature-flag controller going open source 8.

09:26

Juliano Martinez · Adyen

A payments company that processed €1.39 trillion in 2025 on infrastructure it runs itself joined as a Platinum end-user member, and open-sourced kubectl-rexec for auditable kubectl exec in regulated environments 14. The most relevant keynote for anyone under audit.

09:45

A Tribute to Open Source

Anthropic (scheduled speaker: Lucy Sweet)

A thank-you to maintainers. A recap reports 3,000 six-month Claude subscription codes for CNCF project maintainers 8 unverified.

09:52

Code, Containers, and Clusters: The Next Chapter of Apple's Container Framework

Kathryn Baldauf, Vince Prignano · Apple

Container Machine, a persistent Linux environment on the Mac, and a preview Kubernetes plugin that builds local clusters on Apple silicon from upstream kubeadm, kind images and Cilium 1. Each container runs in its own lightweight VM 8. Local clusters that match production get cheaper.

10:11

We Have GitOps at Home: How Meta Went from Terraform on Cron to GitOps for AI Infra

Phong Nguyen, Shaun Hopper · Meta

About 15 engineers run about 100 clusters for AI research and inference. A one-line Helm values change could take a working day in the monolithic Terraform pipeline. Kargo and Argo CD now ship each app on its own and a routine update is live in minutes. Terraform "didn't get fired, just reorged," and app teams fill in an order form instead of writing YAML 1.

10:25

Sovereign AI Starts at the Traffic Layer

Andjelko Iharos · HAProxy Technologies

"Your cluster is sovereign. Your AI isn't." An inference request is plain HTTP with the model name in the JSON body, so the ingress and Gateway API patterns you already run can route it, hash it across GPU backends and enforce policy at the edge 1.

10:32

Built on Kubernetes. Proven at Uber Scale. Now Open Source.

Viv Keswani · Uber

Michelangelo AI, Uber's Kubernetes-native ML platform behind more than 5,000 production models and 45 million predictions per second, launched publicly as open source 1. The code has been on GitHub since May 20, 2026: the Uniflow pipeline framework, API server, controller, local sandbox and CLI 13.

Also on stage: The Flywheel Effect (Syntasso, The Access Group) previewed a Platform Engineering Community White Paper; the TAB and TOC joint keynote (Apple, Defense Unicorns, Lockheed Martin, NVIDIA) on mapping reference architectures to project lifecycle; ScaleOps on autonomous orchestration; Oracle and LiveKit on OKE 1.

Day 3 · Thursday, November 12

Kubernetes as a kernel

The last morning was the most forward-looking. Google and Meta argued that Kubernetes is settling into a lean kernel with purpose-built distributions on top, and that agentic engineering makes building those distributions cheap.

09:12

Ron Kahn, Andrey Velichkevich, Vara Bonthu, Ekin Karabulut, Manabu McCloskey · AWS, Apple, NVIDIA

Kai-Hsun Chen, Hao Jiang · Hark

From the slides: what breaks at 100,000 nodes

Of 34 keynote slots, one posted a deck. It is worth reading even if you will never run a giant cluster, because every fix ships in the versions you are about to upgrade to. The speaker notes frame the choice a managed provider faces at scale: fork it and keep the fix, let customers work around it, or fix it upstream 3.

Slide 2 · Control plane

Kubernetes is designed for 5,000 nodes. Three assumptions broke on the way.

  • 574 ms → 1.7 msAPI server watch cache: writer wait in a 1M-pod benchmark, after replacing one long-held lock with a lazy snapshot (O(N) → O(1))
  • 4× workersHorizontal Pod Autoscaler: a new selector store so reads stop queuing behind writes; on by default in 1.37
  • 300+ pods/sScheduler throughput with a steady pending backlog, against 10 to 20 before a delta-based volume cache

Next: compress objects before they reach etcd (KEP-6264, alpha in 1.38). PRs cited on the slide: watch cache #139928, HPA #139142, scheduler #139238.

Slide 3 · Scheduling

Karpenter now scales DRA workloads from zero

A pod can ask for one GPU and one EFA interface on the same PCIe root. Karpenter 1.15 models those devices on every instance type before launch, so GPU capacity scales from zero with no node groups to declare.

  • 16 providersKarpenter providers that can build on dynamic-capacity DRA, including AWS, Azure, Oracle, Alibaba Cloud, IBM Cloud, Hetzner and Cluster API

EFA driver built on upstream DRANET. Requires Kubernetes 1.34+; GPU sharing 1.36+.

Slide 4 · Node

A 10 GB AI image, pulled in 45 seconds

containerd 2.1 made layer downloads parallel. containerd 2.2 makes unpack parallel too, opt-in, one setting away.

Serial unpack113 s
Parallel unpack45 s

Pull time for a 10 GB vLLM image. containerd #12332 (2.2) and #10177 (2.1), as cited on the slide.

Slide 5 · The asks

Theme 3

The scheduler is where the hard engineering is

DRA went from proposal to the organizing abstraction for devices, with 14 sessions, an NVIDIA driver deep dive and HAMi moving to a DRA-native design 2. Inside it: fractional GPUs, topology-aware placement (a new WG Workload-Aware Scheduling), and quotas that actually hold.

The best talks were honest failure reports. Read these first:

  • Anthropic's Kubernetes Scheduler for Frontier AI: What Worked, What Broke, which opens with "A TPU on the wrong torus coordinate is worthless" 1
  • How Anthropic Almost Melted GKE (And the PRs That Saved It): "At 50,000 nodes, component failure is a statistical certainty" 1
  • Anatomy of an Outage: When Bursty ML Workloads Break the Kubernetes Scheduler (Google, Isomorphic Labs)
  • Why Quotas Are Hard in Kubernetes (VMware by Broadcom)
1.37"Garhwal," released Aug 26, 2026: HPA scale-to-zero went beta, pod-level checkpoint/restore arrived in alpha 16

Theme 4

Platform engineering got a second user: the agent

Platforms were designed around developers self-serving. The open question this year was what an agent may safely do with no human in the loop, which needs machine-readable contracts more than portals. Only about 17 sessions treated human developer experience as the open problem, against 129 that named agents or MCP 2.

  • GitOps is the trust boundary. Meta's keynote, Progressive Delivery for Trust: How Agents Earn the Right to Merge (Transfix: "human review becomes the next scaling bottleneck"), and GitOps Without You Writing YAML (Flux).
  • And the cautionary tale. GitOps Meltdown: How a Commit Triggered a Cascading Delete of 39 Clusters in Production (Nutanix). Automation amplifies whatever you merge.
  • Policy is moving in-tree. Goodbye Webhooks, Hello CEL (Google); Kyverno 1.20 went CEL-first and dropped processing of legacy ClusterPolicy 15.
  • Composition is the agent-facing API. Crossplane functions, kro and Backstage all pitched narrow, typed APIs that an agent can call more safely than raw cloud YAML.

Theme 6

Security: the CVE firehose and the identity gap

Two pains dominated. First, maintainers are drowning in vulnerability reports, increasingly AI-generated: Mythos vs Maintainers: Drowning in the Vulnerability Tsunami (ControlPlane, Docker), AI Found a 0-Day in Metal3. What Happens Next? (Ericsson) 1. A Linux Foundation press briefing reportedly introduced Akrites, a project in which 30+ enterprises triage and patch upstream vulnerabilities 7 unverified.

Second, non-human identity: SPIFFE/SPIRE (13 sessions), a SPIFFE broker API so every Kubernetes object can have an identity, pod certificates and cluster trust bundles, and Coinbase's move From Bearer Tokens to Mesh-Native mTLS 2.

  • Ingress NGINX is retired. Best-effort maintenance ended in March 2026 and the recommended destination is Gateway API 17. Four Resources, One Object (AWS) was the migration talk.
  • New attack surface: prompt injection, poisoned telemetry pipelines, and Why Your Monitoring Stack is a Security Risk (Anthropic, Snowflake).

Theme 7

Observability: OpenTelemetry won, and now it is pointed at AI

OpenTelemetry came up in 38 sessions against 17 for Prometheus by name; it is the default assumption 2. The practitioner talks were about scale and cardinality cost, and the new work is about observing inference and agents.

  • Scale: Tracing at Scale: Running a 16 Billion Spans/Hour Pipeline at Nubank; two-stage OTel aggregation to tame Istio cardinality at 10,000 pods (Yahoo).
  • AI as the subject: The Race to Standardize AI Observability (Google, Microsoft); hallucination detection for observability agents (Apple).
  • AI as the operator, soberly: The SRE Is Not Dead (Best Buy) and a 10-scenario benchmark of open-source LLMs running real Kubernetes operations.

The boring, load-bearing track deserves a mention too: Death by a Thousand Patches: How Pod Status Updates Exhaust etcd (AWS), stale watches in operators, and an 8-minute clock skew that locked a cluster for a day 2.

Eight trends I'd bet on

Early signals, each seen at least once on the keynote stage or in a project launch this week.

Actors replace pods as the agent unit

One session, one sandbox, one gateway, suspended when idle. Packing density is the selling point; identity that survives suspension is the hard part.

Agent Substrate keynote 910

The harness is the runtime

Model, tools, skills and plugins packaged as one deployable unit, moving from laptops to clusters with checkpoint/restore underneath.

Solo.io keynote; K8s 1.37 alpha checkpoint/restore 116

Policy enforced by hardware

OpenShell pushes agent policy down to DPUs. "Ask the agent nicely" is out; enforce it below the kernel.

NVIDIA, Red Hat keynotes 9

Kubernetes as a kernel, with distros

A stable core of API machinery and controllers, and purpose-built distributions on top, made cheaper to build by agentic engineering.

Sovereign inference at the gateway

Bringing it home

What I'd act on first

The full argument, with owners and decisions, lives in GOALS.md. These are the six items the week made most urgent.

  1. Start non-human identity before the agents arrive

    Short-lived workload identity plus fine-grained authorization was the consensus answer. Retrofitting it after agents hold credentials is the painful path.

    GOALS.md §3.6

  2. Make one path to production for people and agents

    GitOps plus policy plus progressive delivery, with traceability for who (or what) authored each change. Let agents propose; let the pipeline decide.

    GOALS.md §1.9, §2.1

  3. Pick a sandbox story before production agents

    Kata or gVisor today; track Agent Substrate and OpenShell as they mature in the CNCF sandbox.

    GOALS.md §3.1, §3.2

  4. Stop upgrading clusters by hand

    Every fleet talk was an upgrade and toil story. Our fleet is growing, so the process has to scale without people.

    GOALS.md §5.2

  5. Budget for OpenTelemetry and cardinality

    Every observability scale story was a sampling and cardinality story, and AI cost tracking rides on the same pipeline.

    GOALS.md §4.2

  6. Move off Ingress NGINX to Gateway API

    It is retired, and the inference routing extensions build on the same data plane, so the migration pays twice.

    ISTIO-VS-GATEWAY-API.md
